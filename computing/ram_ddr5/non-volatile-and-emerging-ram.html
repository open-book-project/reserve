    Chapter 9: Non-volatile and Emerging RAM¶

    These memories keep data without power while still allowing fast, byte-level access. None has replaced DRAM, but several are now standard as embedded memory in microcontrollers and specialised products. This chapter explains how each stores a bit (as a magnetic orientation, a polarisation, a filament, a phase or a nanotube contact), what each is good at, and where each is used or has been tried.

    9.1 MRAM (magnetoresistive RAM)¶

    MRAM stores a bit in a magnetic tunnel junction (MTJ): two magnetic layers separated by a thin insulator. One layer is fixed; the other can point the same way (low resistance, a 0) or the opposite way (high resistance, a 1). Reading senses the resistance; writing flips the free layer.

    STT-MRAM (spin-transfer torque) flips the free layer by passing current through the junction. SOT-MRAM (spin-orbit torque) uses a separate write path for faster, longer-lasting writes and is still largely in research.

    MRAM has very high endurance, is naturally immune to radiation upsets and is offered as embedded memory by major foundries. Uses: embedded code and data memory in microcontrollers, industrial and automotive chips, and aerospace systems.

    9.2 FeRAM / FRAM (ferroelectric RAM)¶

    FeRAM looks like a DRAM cell, but the capacitor is filled with a ferroelectric material whose internal polarisation stays pointing up or down after power is removed. Writes are fast and low in energy, and endurance is very high, on the order of \(10^{14}\) cycles or more. Capacities are small, and reading is destructive, so the cell is rewritten after each read, as in DRAM. Newer hafnium-oxide ferroelectrics may let FeRAM scale further.

    Uses: smart meters, medical devices, data loggers and microcontrollers such as the TI MSP430FR family.

    Example 9.1: How long does a data logger last?

    A data logger writes one record to the same FRAM location once per second. With an endurance of \(10^{14}\) write cycles:

    1. One year has about \(365 \times 24 \times 3600 = 3.15 \times 10^{7}\) seconds, so about \(3.15 \times 10^{7}\) writes per year.
    2. \(10^{14} / 3.15 \times 10^{7} \approx 3.2 \times 10^{6}\) years.

    Endurance is therefore no practical limit for this job. Flash, which wears out after a limited number of writes and is written in large pages, would need wear levelling to survive the same workload.

    9.3 ReRAM / RRAM (resistive RAM)¶

    ReRAM stores a bit as the resistance of a thin oxide layer. Applying a voltage forms or breaks a tiny conductive filament through the oxide, switching between low and high resistance. Cells are simple and can be built between metal layers above the transistors. Endurance is lower than MRAM's.

    Uses: embedded non-volatile memory in microcontrollers at advanced nodes, and research into in-memory computing, where arrays of resistive cells perform multiply-accumulate operations for neural networks directly in the array.

    9.4 PCM and 3D XPoint (phase-change memory)¶

    PCM uses a chalcogenide glass that can be switched by heat pulses between an amorphous state (high resistance) and a crystalline state (low resistance). Intel and Micron's 3D XPoint, sold as Intel Optane, used a related technology in SSDs and in persistent memory DIMMs that sat between DRAM and flash. Despite good performance, Optane was discontinued in 2022 because it could not compete on cost. Phase-change memory still appears as embedded memory in some automotive microcontrollers.

    9.5 NRAM (nanotube RAM)¶

    NRAM stores bits using a layer of carbon nanotubes that are pushed into or out of contact with each other, changing the resistance. It promises speed, endurance and tolerance of heat and radiation, but remains experimental, with no mainstream products as of 2026.

    9.6 Non-volatile RAM summary¶

    Type How it stores a bit Strengths Where used
    MRAM (STT/SOT) Magnetic orientation in a magnetic tunnel junction Fast, very high endurance, radiation-tolerant Embedded MCU memory, industrial, aerospace
    FeRAM / FRAM Polarisation of a ferroelectric capacitor Very low write energy, fast writes, high endurance Smart meters, medical, TI MSP430FR
    ReRAM / RRAM Conductive filament in an oxide (resistance) Simple, dense, built between metal layers Embedded NVM, in-memory compute research
    PCM / 3D XPoint Amorphous versus crystalline phase (resistance) Byte-addressable, denser than DRAM Intel Optane (discontinued 2022), some automotive MCUs
    NRAM Carbon nanotubes in contact or apart Potentially fast and robust Experimental

    The processes on which these are built differ from those of DRAM. Embedded MRAM is typically offered at 28, 22, 16 and 12 nm, moving to 14 and 8 nm; standalone MRAM at 28 to 40 nm; FeRAM at about 130 nm; embedded ReRAM at 40, 28 and 22 nm. Embedded MRAM is available on the GlobalFoundries 22FDX process alongside SRAM (Chapter 16).

    9.7 Summary¶

    • Non-volatile RAMs keep data without power and allow byte access; none has replaced DRAM, but several are standard embedded memories.
    • MRAM stores a bit as a magnetic orientation (high endurance, radiation-tolerant); FeRAM as ferroelectric polarisation (very low write energy, around \(10^{14}\) cycles); ReRAM as a filament's resistance; PCM as amorphous or crystalline phase; NRAM as nanotube contact.
    • Optane, the best-known PCM product, was discontinued in 2022 because of cost; NRAM remains experimental.

    9.8 Exercises¶

    Exercise 9.1. For each memory, name the physical property that represents the bit: MRAM, FeRAM, ReRAM, PCM.

    Answer

    MRAM: the magnetic orientation of the free layer in a magnetic tunnel junction (resistance low or high). FeRAM: the polarisation of a ferroelectric capacitor. ReRAM: the resistance of an oxide, set by forming or breaking a conductive filament. PCM: the phase, amorphous (high resistance) or crystalline (low resistance).

    Exercise 9.2. A smart meter must store a counter updated every 10 seconds for 20 years. How many writes is that, and is an endurance of \(10^{14}\) cycles enough?

    Answer

    Seconds in 20 years \(\approx 20 \times 3.15 \times 10^{7} = 6.3 \times 10^{8}\); one write every 10 s gives \(6.3 \times 10^{7}\) writes, far below \(10^{14}\). Endurance is more than sufficient, by a factor of about \(1.6 \times 10^{6}\).

    Exercise 9.3. Why was Intel Optane discontinued although its performance was good?

    Answer

    It could not compete on cost. It had sat between DRAM and flash in persistent-memory DIMMs and SSDs, but good performance alone did not make up for the cost.

    Exercise 9.4. Which non-volatile RAM would you consider for (a) a spacecraft microcontroller's code memory; (b) in-memory neural-network computing research; (c) a medical device logging data frequently at very low energy?

    Answer

    (a) MRAM, because it is radiation-tolerant and endurant. (b) ReRAM, whose resistive arrays can perform multiply-accumulate directly. (c) FeRAM, with very low write energy and very high endurance.

    Exercise 9.5. Explain what "destructive read" means for FeRAM and why DRAM shares the property.

    Answer

    Reading an FeRAM cell disturbs the polarisation that stores the bit, so the controller must rewrite the value after every read. A DRAM cell shares its charge with the bitline when read, which drains the capacitor, so DRAM also rewrites the row after reading (Section 2.3).
