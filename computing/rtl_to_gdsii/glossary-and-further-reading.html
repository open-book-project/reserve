    Glossary and Further Reading¶

    Glossary¶

    Antenna effect
    Damage to a thin gate oxide by charge that a long, still-unconnected metal wire collects from the plasma during fabrication. Cured by layer jumping or an antenna diode. See Chapter 6.
    ASIC
    Application-specific integrated circuit. In this book, a standard-cell ASIC built from a library of ready-made cells.
    ATPG
    Automatic test-pattern generation: computing the input vectors that expose manufacturing defects, usually under the stuck-at fault model.
    Blockage
    A region of the core where placement of cells is forbidden or density-limited.
    Buffer
    A cell that re-drives a signal without changing its logic value, used to break up long or heavily loaded nets or to add delay on a hold-critical path.
    Clock gating
    Switching the clock off to idle register banks to save power, using integrated clock-gate (ICG) cells.
    Clock-tree synthesis (CTS)
    Building a balanced, buffered network that delivers the clock to every flip-flop at nearly the same time. See Chapter 5.
    Clock uncertainty
    A margin subtracted from setup and hold slack for clock jitter and safety.
    Core
    The inner region of the die in which standard cells and macros are placed and routed.
    Corner
    A named combination of process, voltage and temperature at which cell delays are characterised: slow (SS), typical (TT) or fast (FF).
    CPPR (CRPR)
    Clock-path pessimism removal: crediting back pessimism counted twice on the part of the clock path that launch and capture share.
    Critical path
    The timing path with the smallest slack; it sets the maximum clock frequency.
    DEF
    Design Exchange Format: the file that records the placement and routing of a design.
    Density fill
    Non-functional dummy metal added after routing so that every window of every layer meets the foundry's minimum and maximum metal density.
    DFT
    Design for test: inserting structures, chiefly scan chains, that make a manufactured chip controllable and observable.
    Die
    The whole piece of silicon, edge to edge, including the margin around the core.
    DRC
    Design rule check: comparing every shape of the layout with the foundry's geometric rules. See Chapter 8.
    Drive strength
    The size of a cell's transistors, written _1, _2, _4 or ×1, ×2, ×4: bigger cells drive larger loads faster but cost area, power and input capacitance.
    Electromigration (EM)
    The slow erosion of metal under sustained high current density, which can finally open a wire.
    ERC
    Electrical rule check: finding floating gates, power-to-ground shorts, wrongly tied wells and missing taps.
    Floorplan
    The first physical stage: the die and core size, aspect ratio, pins, macro positions and the power grid.
    FPGA
    Field-programmable gate array: a reprogrammable fabric of look-up tables and other blocks.
    g-cell
    A coarse routing tile used by global routing and by congestion maps, many tracks wide.
    GDSII
    Graphic Design System II: the industry-standard binary stream format for a finished layout, pure geometry by layer.
    Halo
    A keep-out guard band around a hard macro in which no standard cells are placed.
    Hold time, hold slack
    The time for which data must stay steady after the clock edge, and the margin by which a path meets that requirement. Independent of the clock period.
    HPWL
    Half-perimeter wirelength: the width plus the height of the box enclosing all the pins of a net.
    IR drop
    The voltage lost across the resistance of the power grid, \(V = I \times R\).
    Latency (insertion delay)
    The delay from the clock source to a flip-flop's clock pin.
    LEC
    Logic equivalence checking: a formal proof that two designs, such as the RTL and the final netlist, compute the same function.
    LEF
    Library Exchange Format: the physical abstract of cells (outline, pins, blockages) and the technology (metal layers, vias, rules).
    Legalisation
    The placement phase that removes overlaps and snaps cells to rows and sites.
    Liberty (.lib)
    The characterised timing and power model of a cell library, one file per corner.
    LVS
    Layout versus schematic: extracting a netlist from the layout and proving that it matches the intended netlist.
    MCMM
    Multi-corner multi-mode analysis: timing every functional mode at every relevant corner.
    Net
    The logical connection of a set of pins in a netlist. The physical metal that realises it is its wire.
    OASIS
    Open Artwork System Interchange Standard: a compact successor to GDSII.
    OCV
    On-chip variation: the small differences between nominally identical cells on one die, modelled by derating.
    OpenLANE
    The open-source orchestrator that runs Yosys, OpenROAD, Magic, KLayout and netgen as one RTL-to-GDSII flow.
    PDK
    Process design kit: a foundry's design rules, device models, layer definitions and standard-cell library for one process.
    PDN
    Power distribution network: the rings, straps, rails and vias that carry VDD and VSS to every cell.
    Pitch
    The centre-to-centre distance between neighbouring routing tracks.
    PPA
    Power, performance, area: the three goals that pull against one another.
    PVT
    Process, voltage and temperature: the three variables that set a cell's delay.
    RTL
    Register-transfer level: a description of a circuit as registers and the logic between them.
    Scan chain
    Scan flip-flops linked into a shift register so that a tester can set and read every register.
    SDC
    Synopsys Design Constraints: the Tcl dialect that states clocks, input and output delays and timing exceptions.
    Setup time, setup slack
    The time for which data must be steady before the clock edge, and the margin by which a path meets its deadline. Depends on the clock period.
    Site
    The unit grid of a row; a cell's width is a whole number of sites.
    Skew
    The difference in clock arrival between two flip-flops: capture latency minus launch latency.
    Slack
    Required time minus arrival time. Negative slack is a violation.
    SPEF
    Standard Parasitic Exchange Format: the extracted resistance and capacitance of every net.
    Standard cell
    A pre-designed, pre-characterised logic gate of fixed height that the flow places in rows.
    STA
    Static timing analysis: exhaustive, vector-less checking of every timing path.
    Streaming out
    Writing the final layout as a GDSII or OASIS file.
    Tapeout
    The moment the final mask data is sent to the foundry.
    Timing arc
    A characterised delay from one input pin to one output pin of a cell, tabulated against input slew and output load.
    Timing closure
    Making every timing path meet its constraint at every corner and mode.
    TNS, WNS
    Total and worst negative slack: the sum of all negative slacks, and the single most negative one.
    Track
    A legal centre line for a wire on a routing layer.
    Utilisation
    Total standard-cell area divided by core area.
    Via
    A small metal plug joining a wire on one layer to a wire on the next.
    Wire-load model
    A statistical estimate of a net's capacitance from its fan-out, used before real wires exist.

    Further reading¶

    The tools and the process kit of this book are documented by their own projects, and the documentation is the place to settle any detail of a command or variable that changes between versions.

    • OpenLANE: the flow reference, every configuration variable and many example designs, at openlane.readthedocs.io, with the sources at github.com/The-OpenROAD-Project/OpenLane (the classic flow) and github.com/efabless/openlane2 (the Python rewrite).
    • OpenROAD: the place-and-route and timing engine, with an interactive Tcl command reference, at openroad.readthedocs.io and theopenroadproject.org.
    • The SkyWater Sky130 open process kit: layers, cells and rules, at skywater-pdk.readthedocs.io and github.com/google/skywater-pdk.
    • Matt Venn's "Zero to ASIC" course (zerotoasiccourse.com) and Tiny Tapeout (tinytapeout.com): the quickest route from a first design to a chip that is actually made.
    • Elsewhere in this library, the field guide ASIC Design: RTL to GDSII covers the same flow in plain language with a wider view of tools, standards and careers, and the companion books RTL Design and Hardware Description Languages and Microarchitecture and SoC Design describe the designs that enter this flow.
